## What it does

The discharge chamber is where propellant becomes plasma. Electrons from the hollow cathode are carried into the chamber along with the propellant; when an electron hits an argon atom with enough energy it knocks an electron loose, and the more collisions there are, the more of the gas is ionized. The ions then drift toward the negatively charged grids of the ion optics, which accelerate them out as thrust.

ION-I's first thruster uses **argon** instead of the xenon most flight thrusters use, and for now it has **no separate neutralizing cathode**.

## System outline

- **Magnetic rings** around the chamber create a field strong enough to confine electrons and keep them off the chamber walls. Electrons stay in the chamber longer and ionize more argon.
- An **aluminum chamber** contains the ions and electrons.
- A **propellant injector** feeds argon in at a constant, controlled rate.
